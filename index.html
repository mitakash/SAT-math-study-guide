1. Heart of Algebra

▼
Solving Linear Equations (One Variable)
This is the most fundamental skill in algebra. You'll need to isolate a variable in equations that may include constants, fractions, and the distributive property.
Khan Academy: Solving Linear Equations and Inequalities

Example Problem

If (3/5)x + 2 = 14, what is the value of x?
Solution:
  1. Subtract 2 from both sides: (3/5)x = 12
  2. Multiply both sides by the reciprocal, 5/3: x = 12 × 5/3
  3. Solve: x = 60/3
  4. Answer: x = 20
Systems of Linear Equations
These are very common on the SAT. You'll be given two linear equations with two variables (x and y) and asked to find the x-value, the y-value, or the sum x+y that solves both equations.
Khan Academy: Systems of Linear Equations Word Problems

Example Problem

2x + 3y = 12
x - 4y = -5
Based on the system of equations above, what is the value of x + y?
Solution:
  1. Isolate a variable (Substitution Method): Solve the second equation for x: x = 4y - 5
  2. Substitute: Plug this expression for x into the first equation: 2(4y - 5) + 3y = 12
  3. Solve for y:
    • 8y - 10 + 3y = 12
    • 11y - 10 = 12
    • 11y = 22
    • y = 2
  4. Solve for x: Plug y=2 back into your isolated equation: x = 4(2) - 5 = 8 - 5 = 3
  5. Answer the question: The question asks for x + y. 3 + 2 = 5
  6. Answer: 5
Graphing Linear Equations
You need to understand the relationship between a linear equation (y = mx + b) and its graph in the xy-plane. This includes identifying the slope (m) and the y-intercept (b).
Khan Academy: Graphs of Linear Equations and Functions

Example Problem

3x + 2y = 6
A line in the xy-plane is represented by the equation above. What is the slope of the line?
Solution:
  1. To find the slope, convert the equation into slope-intercept form y = mx + b
  2. Subtract 3x from both sides: 2y = -3x + 6
  3. Divide both sides by 2: y = -3/2 x + 3
  4. The slope (m) is the coefficient of x
  5. Answer: -3/2
Solving Linear Inequalities
These are solved just like linear equations, with one key difference: you must flip the inequality sign if you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number. These are often presented as word problems.
Khan Academy: Linear Inequality Word Problems

Example Problem

If -4x + 7 ≤ 19, which of the following represents all possible values of x?
A) x ≥ 3
B) x ≤ 3
C) x ≥ -3
D) x ≤ -3
Solution:
  1. Subtract 7 from both sides: -4x ≤ 12
  2. Divide both sides by -4. Because you are dividing by a negative number, you must flip the inequality sign
  3. x ≥ 12/(-4)
  4. x ≥ -3
  5. Answer: C) x ≥ -3
Absolute Value Equations
Absolute value equations typically have two possible solutions. You must set up two separate equations: one where the expression inside the absolute value equals the positive result, and one where it equals the negative result.
Khan Academy: Solving Absolute Value Equations

Example Problem

What is one possible solution to the equation |3x - 7| = 2?
Solution:
  1. Set up two equations:
    • Case 1: 3x - 7 = 2
    • Case 2: 3x - 7 = -2
  2. Solve Case 1:
    • 3x = 9
    • x = 3
  3. Solve Case 2:
    • 3x = 5
    • x = 5/3
  4. Answer: Both 3 and 5/3 are correct solutions. You would enter either one.

2. Problem Solving and Data Analysis

▼
Ratios, Rates, and Proportions
The SAT loves to present problems where you must find a value using a given ratio or rate. This includes unit conversions (e.g., feet to miles) and scaling recipes or populations.
Khan Academy: Ratios, Rates, and Proportions

Example Problem

A recipe for 12 cookies requires 3/4 cup of sugar. How many cups of sugar are needed to make 30 cookies?
Solution:
  1. Set up a proportion: Let x be the amount of sugar needed for 30 cookies
    sugar/cookies = (3/4)/12 = x/30
  2. Cross-multiply: 12 × x = (3/4) × 30
  3. Solve for x:
    • 12x = 90/4
    • 12x = 22.5
    • x = 22.5/12
  4. Simplify: x = 1.875 or 1 7/8
  5. Answer: 1 7/8 cups of sugar
Percentages
You'll need to fluently calculate percentages, percent increases/decreases, and use percentages to find parts of a whole (e.g., "What is 15% of 60?") or the whole itself (e.g., "15 is 30% of what number?").
Khan Academy: Percentages

Example Problem

A store's regular price for a jacket is $60. The store offers a 20% discount. An 8% sales tax is then applied to the discounted price. What is the final cost of the jacket?
Solution:
  1. Calculate the discount: 20% of $60 is 0.20 × 60 = $12
  2. Find the discounted price: $60 - $12 = $48
  3. Calculate the sales tax: 8% of the discounted price ($48) is 0.08 × 48 = $3.84
  4. Find the final cost: Add the tax to the discounted price: $48 + $3.84 = $51.84
  5. Answer: $51.84
  6. Alternative Method: Multiply $60 by (1 - 0.20) to get $48. Then multiply $48 by (1 + 0.08) to get $51.84
Reading Tables and Graphs (Scatterplots, Bar Graphs)
You will be presented with data in various formats and asked to read values, find relationships (like a "line of best fit" on a scatterplot), or calculate values based on the data.
Khan Academy: Scatterplots

Example Problem

The scatterplot shows the number of hours 10 students studied for an exam and the score they received. A line of best fit is also drawn. What is the predicted score for a student who studies for 3 hours, according to the line of best fit?
Solution:
  1. Locate the value on the x-axis: Find "3 hours" on the horizontal axis
  2. Move to the line: Trace vertically up from "3 hours" until you hit the line of best fit (not just any data point, but the line itself)
  3. Read the y-value: Trace horizontally from that point on the line over to the vertical y-axis (which represents the score)
  4. Estimate the value: If the line at x=3 corresponds to a y-value of 75...
  5. Answer: The predicted score is 75
Statistics (Mean, Median, Mode)
You must know the definitions of mean (average), median (middle value), and mode (most frequent value) and be able to calculate them from a set of data. SAT problems often ask how adding or removing a data point affects these values.
Khan Academy: Mean, Median, and Mode

Example Problem

A class's test scores are: 70, 75, 80, 80, 85, 90, 100. What is the median score?
Solution:
  1. Order the data: The data is already in ascending order
  2. Find the middle value: There are 7 data points. The middle value is the 4th one (3 before it, 3 after it)
  3. Identify the median: The 4th value in the list is 80
  4. Answer: The median score is 80
  5. Note: If there were an even number of data points (e.g., 6), the median would be the average of the two middle values (the 3rd and 4th)

3. Passport to Advanced Math

▼
Solving Quadratic Equations
These are extremely common. You must be able to solve ax² + bx + c = 0 problems, either by factoring (when possible) or by using the quadratic formula.
Khan Academy: Solving Quadratic Equations

Example Problem

What is one possible solution to the equation x² + 6x - 16 = 0?
Solution:
  1. Try to factor: We need two numbers that multiply to -16 and add to +6. These numbers are +8 and -2
  2. Write the factored form: (x + 8)(x - 2) = 0
  3. Find the solutions: The equation is true if x + 8 = 0 or if x - 2 = 0
  4. The two solutions are x = -8 and x = 2
  5. Answer: You could enter either 2 or -8
Graphing Parabolas (Vertex Form & Intercepts)
The SAT will test your knowledge of parabolas (the graph of a quadratic function). You need to know that the vertex is the minimum or maximum point and how to find x- and y-intercepts.
Khan Academy: Graphing Parabolas in Vertex Form

Example Problem

f(x) = (x - 3)² + 5
The graph of the function f in the xy-plane is a parabola. What are the coordinates of the vertex of the parabola?
Solution:
  1. The function is given in vertex form, which is y = a(x - h)² + k
  2. In this form, the vertex of the parabola is at the point (h, k)
  3. In the given equation, h = 3 and k = 5
  4. Answer: The vertex is at (3, 5)
Function Notation (f(x))
This is the language of advanced algebra. You'll be asked to evaluate functions (find f(3)), solve for a variable (find x if f(x) = 3), or combine functions (find f(g(x))).
Khan Academy: SAT Worked Example - Function Notation

Example Problem

f(x) = 2x² - 5
g(x) = x + 3
What is the value of f(g(2))?
Solution:
  1. This is a composite function. Always work from the inside out. First, find g(2)
  2. g(2) = (2) + 3 = 5
  3. Now, use this result as the input for f. Find f(5)
  4. f(5) = 2(5)² - 5
  5. f(5) = 2(25) - 5
  6. f(5) = 50 - 5 = 45
  7. Answer: 45
Manipulating Polynomials
This involves adding, subtracting, or multiplying complex algebraic expressions. The key is to correctly distribute and combine like terms.
Khan Academy: Adding and Subtracting Polynomials

Example Problem

Which of the following expressions is equivalent to (5x² - 3) - (2x² - 4x + 2)?
A) 3x² + 4x - 5
B) 3x² - 4x - 1
C) 7x² - 4x - 1
D) 7x² + 4x - 5
Solution:
  1. Distribute the negative sign to every term in the second parenthesis: 5x² - 3 - 2x² + 4x - 2
  2. Combine like terms (the x² terms): 5x² - 2x² = 3x²
  3. Combine like terms (the x terms): There is only one, +4x
  4. Combine like terms (the constants): -3 - 2 = -5
  5. Put it all together: 3x² + 4x - 5
  6. Answer: A)
Exponential Functions (Growth & Decay)
You'll see these in word problems about compound interest, population growth, or radioactive decay. You must understand what each part of the formula y = a(b)^x represents.
Khan Academy: Interpreting Exponential Functions

Example Problem

A car's value V depreciates according to the function V(t) = 18,000(0.85)^t, where t is the number of years since it was purchased. What does the number 0.85 represent in this function?
Solution:
  1. The general form is A = P(1 ± r)^t, where P is the initial amount and r is the rate of change
  2. Here, the number 18,000 is the initial value of the car
  3. The base, 0.85, represents the rate of decay. Since 0.85 = 1 - 0.15, it means the car loses 15% of its value each year
  4. Answer: The car's value decreases by 15% each year (or it retains 85% of its value each year)

4. Additional Topics in Math

▼
Equation of a Circle
You'll be expected to know the standard form of a circle's equation and use it to find the circle's center and radius, or vice-versa. The standard equation of a circle is (x - h)² + (y - k)² = r², where (h, k) is the center of the circle and r is the radius.
Khan Academy: Worked Example - Equation of a Circle

Example Problem

x² + 8x + y² - 6y = 11
The equation above describes a circle in the xy-plane. What is the radius of the circle?
Solution:
  1. This equation is not in standard form. We must complete the square for both x and y
  2. Group terms: (x² + 8x) + (y² - 6y) = 11
  3. Complete the square for x: Take half of the x-coefficient (8), which is 4. Square it (4² = 16). Add 16 to both sides
    (x² + 8x + 16) + (y² - 6y) = 11 + 16
  4. Complete the square for y: Take half of the y-coefficient (-6), which is -3. Square it ((-3)² = 9). Add 9 to both sides
    (x² + 8x + 16) + (y² - 6y + 9) = 11 + 16 + 9
  5. Factor the perfect squares: (x + 4)² + (y - 3)² = 36
  6. Find the radius: The equation is now in standard form. r² = 36
  7. Answer: The radius r is √36, which is 6
Right-Triangle Trigonometry (SOH CAH TOA)
You'll need to find the sine, cosine, or tangent of an angle in a right triangle given its side lengths, or find a side length given an angle. SOH CAH TOA reminds us: Sin = Opposite/Hypotenuse, Cos = Adjacent/Hypotenuse, Tan = Opposite/Adjacent.
Khan Academy: SAT Worked Example - SOH CAH TOA

Example Problem

In a right triangle, the length of side AB is 13 and the length of side BC is 5. What is the cosine of angle A?
Solution:
  1. SOH CAH TOA reminds us that Cosine = Adjacent/Hypotenuse
  2. Identify the sides:
    • The angle is angle A
    • The Hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle, which is AB = 13
    • The Opposite side (opposite angle A) is BC = 5
    • The Adjacent side (next to angle A) is AC
  3. Find the missing side: We need the Adjacent side (AC). Use the Pythagorean theorem: a² + b² = c²
    • (AC)² + (BC)² = (AB)²
    • (AC)² + 5² = 13²
    • (AC)² + 25 = 169
    • (AC)² = 144
    • AC = 12 (This is a 5-12-13 special right triangle)
  4. Calculate Cosine: cos(A) = Adjacent/Hypotenuse = AC/AB = 12/13
  5. Answer: 12/13
Radians, Arcs, and Sectors
You must be able to convert between degrees and radians and use the formula Arc Length = radius × angle (in radians) or degrees/360 = arc length/circumference.
Khan Academy: Degrees to Radians Khan Academy: Arc Length from Angle (Radians)

Example Problem

A circle has a radius of 10. What is the length of an arc intercepted by a central angle of 2π/5 radians?
Solution:
  1. Use the formula Arc Length = r × θ, where r is the radius and θ is the central angle in radians
  2. Plug in the values:
    • r = 10
    • θ = 2π/5
  3. Calculate: Arc Length = 10 × 2π/5
  4. Arc Length = 20π/5
  5. Arc Length = 4π
  6. Answer: 4π
Complex Numbers
You'll need to perform basic operations (add, subtract, multiply) with complex numbers. The most important rule to know is that i = √(-1), which means i² = -1.
Khan Academy: Introduction to Complex Numbers

Example Problem 1: Addition

For i = √(-1), what is the sum (5 + 3i) + (-2 + 7i)?
Solution:
  1. Treat i just like you would treat a variable (x)
  2. Combine like terms: Combine the real parts and the imaginary parts separately
  3. Real parts: 5 + (-2) = 3
  4. Imaginary parts: 3i + 7i = 10i
  5. Combine the results: 3 + 10i
  6. Answer: 3 + 10i

Example Problem 2: Multiplication

What is (4 - 2i)(3 + 5i) expressed in the form a + bi?
Solution:
  1. Use the FOIL method (First, Outer, Inner, Last)
  2. First: 4 × 3 = 12
  3. Outer: 4 × 5i = 20i
  4. Inner: -2i × 3 = -6i
  5. Last: -2i × 5i = -10i²
  6. Combine: 12 + 20i - 6i - 10i²
  7. Simplify: Combine the i terms: 12 + 14i - 10i²
  8. Crucial Step: Remember that i² = -1. Substitute this in: 12 + 14i - 10(-1)
  9. 12 + 14i + 10
  10. Combine real parts: 12 + 10 = 22
  11. Answer: 22 + 14i
